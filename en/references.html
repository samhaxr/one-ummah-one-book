References

Every verse and hadith used on the site, with full references and links to the original sources

Method and sources

Source rule: all the material on this site is taken from only three sources: the Quran, Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. No other hadith collection, tafsir or scholar's statement is quoted. The explanatory English text (mirroring the Urdu) serves only to connect the texts to one another and tries to stay close to their wording.

An organising frame: the division into lessons, the headings, the sections of each lesson, and the branches and stages of the diagrams (for example "one rope or scattered factions", "the chain of revelation", "the causes of division", "the path of decline", "the mirror", "the checklist") are only an organising frame for explanation. This grouping is not itself part of the texts, nor does it claim to be a technical or juristic classification. Verses about the People of the Book or earlier nations are cited as a warning for this ummah, not to pass judgement on any particular Muslim group. The actual evidence for every point is the verse or hadith given with it.

The Quran: the Arabic text is in the Uthmani script (quran-uthmani). The English translation is Saheeh International (en.sahih); the Urdu version uses the translation of Maulana Muhammad Junagarhi (ur.junagarhi). All were fetched from api.alquran.cloud, and the surah and verse number of every verse were checked. That source joins the basmalah to the start of the first verse of each surah; here it has been separated from the verse text (except in al-Fatihah). References are given as "Surah name, surah:verse" and each one links to quran.com.

Hadith: the Arabic text of each hadith (with its isnad) is taken from the corresponding page on sunnah.com. The English translation is the one published on that same sunnah.com page. The Urdu version's translation comes from the urd-bukhari / urd-muslim editions of fawazahmed0/hadith-api, checked against both the number and the content of each hadith. Every Arabic phrase quoted in «» in the lesson text was extracted word for word from the source text automatically when the site was built, and re-checked by a separate verification step. English hadith translations are reproduced as published, including their original spelling.

Urdu version (اردو): the site is also available in Urdu (click «اردو» at the top). The Arabic text is identical in both versions.

Hadith numbering: all numbers follow the sunnah.com "Reference" numbering (Sahih al-Bukhari: the common standard numbering; Sahih Muslim: the sunnah.com reference number, which generally matches the numbering of Muhammad Fu'ad 'Abd al-Baqi). Where sunnah.com lists several narrations under one number, the link points to that specific narration, for example Sahih Muslim 1715a; its Urdu translation is listed in the fawazahmed0 edition as 1715.01. The Arabic text of every hadith was matched between sunnah.com and fawazahmed0 so that the Urdu translation belongs to the same narration.

Companions' words versus the Prophet's ﷺ words: in Sahih al-Bukhari 118 the remark about the verse on concealment is Abu Hurayrah's own; Sahih al-Bukhari 7363 is a statement of Ibn 'Abbas; in Sahih al-Bukhari 3456 and Sahih Muslim 2669 "the Jews and the Christians?" is the Companions' question; in Sahih al-Bukhari 7084 the questions are Hudhayfah's; in Sahih al-Bukhari 3267 and Sahih Muslim 2989 the opening conversation is Usama ibn Zayd's; in Sahih al-Bukhari 3346 the question is Zaynab bint Jahsh's; in Sahih al-Bukhari 4119 the two views are the Companions' own, and in Sahih al-Bukhari 2410 the account is Ibn Mas'ud's, where the narrator Shu'bah says "I think" about the words "do not dispute". Sahih al-Bukhari 6502 and Sahih Muslim 2577 are hadith qudsi (Allah's words, conveyed by the Prophet ﷺ). These distinctions are also made in the lesson text.

Material not included: the well-known report that "the ummah will split into seventy-three sects" was not found in Sahih al-Bukhari or Sahih Muslim (the full texts of both were searched for the Arabic words "ثلاث وسبعين" and "اثنتين وسبعين"), so it is not included. Likewise the report of 'Adi ibn Hatim explaining at-Tawbah 9:31 ("they took their scholars as lords") is not in these two books, so only the verse itself is used. Sahih al-Bukhari 435 and 436 (taking graves as places of prostration) are listed together on sunnah.com and their Urdu translation could not be separated with certainty, so Sahih Muslim 531 on the same subject was used instead. Sahih Muslim 1770 (the Banu Qurayzah episode) mentions Zuhr where Sahih al-Bukhari 4119 mentions 'Asr; to avoid confusion only the Bukhari narration is given.

Names in the English translation: the sunnah.com English translation of Sahih Muslim 2666 names the narrator "Abdullah b. Umar", while the Arabic text has 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr; and for Sahih al-Bukhari 5060 the English has only "Abdullah", while the Arabic has Jundub ibn 'Abdullah. The lesson text follows the Arabic; the English translation is reproduced as published.

Totals: altogether 148 verses and 87 hadith are used on this site (lessons, diagrams and quizzes).
